Nissan Skyline GT-R R32

The Nissan Skyline GT-R R32 was introduced in 1989 and quickly became a Japanese performance icon. It was powered by the famous RB26DETT, a 2.6-liter twin-turbocharged inline-six engine known for its strength and tuning potential. The R32 also featured Nissan’s ATTESA E-TS all-wheel-drive system and Super-HICAS four-wheel steering, giving it excellent traction and handling. The R32 became especially famous through motorsport, where it dominated touring-car racing and earned the nickname “Godzilla.” Its combination of advanced technology, lightweight construction, and powerful engine made it extremely competitive. Today, the R32 GT-R is highly respected by car enthusiasts and collectors. It established the foundation for the modern GT-R legend.

Nissan Skyline GT-R R33

The Nissan Skyline GT-R R33 was released in 1995 as the successor to the R32. It used the same RB26DETT 2.6-liter twin-turbocharged inline-six engine but featured improvements to its chassis, suspension, and all-wheel-drive system. Compared with the R32, the R33 was larger and more refined, making it more comfortable while still offering impressive performance. Its advanced ATTESA E-TS all-wheel-drive and Super-HICAS four-wheel-steering systems helped provide excellent stability and cornering ability. The R33 also became known for its Nürburgring performance, proving that it was capable of competing with some of the world's best performance cars. Although initially less popular than the R32, the R33 has become increasingly appreciated by enthusiasts and collectors.

Nissan Skyline GT-R R34

The Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 debuted in 1999 and became one of the most famous Japanese sports cars ever made. It was powered by the RB26DETT 2.6-liter twin-turbocharged inline-six and featured advanced ATTESA E-TS all-wheel drive. Its aggressive styling, iconic circular taillights, and advanced technology made the R34 instantly recognizable. The car also featured a multifunction display that showed various engine and performance statistics. Special versions such as the V-Spec and V-Spec II added improved suspension, aerodynamics, and performance-focused equipment. The R34 became even more famous through movies and video games, especially the Fast & Furious franchise. Today, it is considered a legendary Japanese performance car and remains highly desirable among collectors and enthusiasts.

Nissan GT-R R35

The Nissan GT-R R35 was introduced in 2007 and became the first GT-R to drop the Skyline name. It featured a completely new design and was powered by the VR38DETT, a 3.8-liter twin-turbocharged V6 engine. Unlike earlier GT-Rs, the R35 used a six-speed dual-clutch transmission, allowing extremely fast gear changes. Its advanced all-wheel-drive system helped deliver remarkable acceleration and cornering performance. The R35 became famous for offering supercar-level performance at a lower price than many European rivals. Nissan continued improving it over the years, producing versions such as the Track Edition and high-performance GT-R Nismo. With its technology, speed, and distinctive design, the R35 became one of the most recognizable modern Japanese performance cars.
