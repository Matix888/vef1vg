TOYOTA

History of Toyota

Toyota's origins go back to 1937, when Kiichiro Toyoda established Toyota Motor Co. after his family's successful textile-machinery business. Toyota initially focused on affordable mass-produced cars and expanded rapidly after World War II. The company became internationally famous through models such as the Corolla, launched in 1966, and later the Camry, Land Cruiser, Supra, and Hilux. Toyota's manufacturing philosophy, particularly the Toyota Production System, also became hugely influential worldwide through its emphasis on efficiency, quality control, and reducing waste.

Current State of Toyota

Today, Toyota is the world's largest automaker by annual vehicle sales, producing cars under Toyota and Lexus as well as smaller vehicles through partnerships and subsidiaries. Its lineup ranges from small cars such as the Yaris to SUVs, pickups, hybrids, luxury vehicles, and performance cars. Toyota has invested heavily in hybrid technology, beginning with the Prius in 1997, while also developing battery-electric, plug-in hybrid, and hydrogen vehicles. The company continues to pursue multiple powertrain technologies rather than relying exclusively on one type of vehicle, with hybrids remaining a particularly important part of its global strategy.

Most Popular Models

Toyota's most important models include the Corolla, RAV4, Camry, Hilux, Land Cruiser, Yaris, and Prius. The Corolla is one of the best-selling nameplates in automotive history, while the RAV4 has become one of Toyota's most important global vehicles as demand for SUVs has grown. The Hilux is particularly well known for durability and is extremely popular in many international markets, while the Land Cruiser has developed a reputation for serious off-road capability. Among enthusiasts, the GR Supra, GR Yaris, GR Corolla, and GR86 represent Toyota's modern performance lineup.

Toyota Motorsports

Toyota has an extensive motorsport history covering Formula 1, the World Rally Championship, the World Endurance Championship, NASCAR, Super GT, and the Dakar Rally. Its biggest recent success has come through Toyota Gazoo Racing, which has dominated the World Rally Championship and achieved major endurance-racing success with the TS050 and GR010 Hybrid. Toyota has won the 24 Hours of Le Mans repeatedly in the modern era, while its rally program has produced multiple manufacturers' and drivers' championships. The company's motorsport division also directly influences road cars, particularly the GR performance range, making Toyota one of the Japanese manufacturers most actively connecting factory racing with production vehicles.
