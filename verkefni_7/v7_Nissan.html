NISSAN

History of Nissan

Nissan's roots go back to 1911, when Kwaishinsha Motor Car Works was established in Tokyo and later produced the DAT automobile; the Nissan name itself emerged in the 1930s. The company expanded rapidly after World War II, with the Datsun brand becoming internationally recognized. During the 1960s–90s, Nissan developed some of Japan's most famous cars, including the Skyline, Fairlady Z, Silvia, and Patrol. The company also became known for advanced engines and performance technology, eventually creating NISMO in 1984 as its dedicated motorsport and performance division.

Current State of Nissan

Today, Nissan is one of Japan's major automakers and is part of the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance. As of March 2026, Nissan employed about 120,000 people and sold approximately 3.15 million vehicles globally. The company is currently restructuring its business through its Re:Nissan strategy, aiming for a leaner model lineup while expanding its electrification and hybrid technology. Its current strategy includes EVs such as the LEAF and Ariya alongside hybrids and e-POWER models, while Nissan continues to develop traditional gasoline-powered vehicles in important markets.

Most Popular Models

Nissan's major models today include the Rogue/X-Trail, Qashqai, Patrol, Sentra, Frontier, and LEAF, although the exact lineup varies by country. The Rogue/X-Trail family is particularly important to Nissan's global volume, while the Patrol is a major SUV in markets such as the Middle East. The LEAF was one of the world's earliest mass-market EVs and remains an important part of Nissan's electrification history. For enthusiasts, however, the most famous Nissan models are the GT-R, Z, Skyline GT-R, Silvia, and 370Z, which established Nissan as one of Japan's major performance-car manufacturers. Nissan's current product strategy specifically identifies the Z and Patrol as "heartbeat" models representing the company's heritage and emotional appeal.

Nissan Motorsports

Nissan has a deep motorsport history covering Super GT, Formula E, endurance racing, rallying, touring cars, and sports-car racing. The company's NISMO division was created in 1984 and became responsible for much of Nissan's factory racing and performance development. Nissan achieved major international recognition with the Skyline GT-R, while its racing history also includes success in events such as the Safari Rally and the 24 Hours of Daytona. Today, NISMO continues to compete with the Nissan Z in Super GT and Nissan operates a factory Formula E team; Nissan entered Formula E in 2018 and is the only Japanese automaker to have competed in the championship since its fifth season. NISMO also continues to transfer racing technology into road cars such as the Z NISMO and Patrol NISMO.
