HONDA

History of Honda

Honda was founded in Japan in 1948 by Soichiro Honda and initially focused on motorcycles, quickly becoming known for innovative, high-revving engines and racing success. It entered automobile production in 1963 and launched the Civic in 1972, followed by the Accord in 1976. During the 1980s and 1990s, Honda developed its reputation for engineering through technologies such as VTEC, performance models like the Integra Type R and Civic Type R, and the NSX sports car. Honda also expanded globally, creating Acura in the United States and becoming one of the world's largest motorcycle and automobile manufacturers

Current State of Honda

Today, Honda is a global company producing automobiles, motorcycles, engines, and other mobility products. Motorcycles remain an enormous part of the business, with Honda selling more than 20 million motorcycles annually worldwide. In automobiles, its major focus is on models such as the Civic, CR-V, Accord, and HR-V, while the company is transitioning toward electrification. As of 2026, Honda has adjusted its strategy to place greater emphasis on hybrids in the near term while continuing development of EVs, batteries, software, and advanced driver-assistance technology.

Most Popular Models

Honda's best-known and most successful models include the Civic, CR-V, Accord, and HR-V. The Civic, introduced in 1972, has sold more than 27 million units globally and remains one of Honda's defining cars, available in practical versions as well as high-performance Si and Type R variants. The CR-V has become one of Honda's most important modern vehicles, passing 15 million global sales in 2025. The Accord has historically been a major midsize sedan, particularly in North America, while the HR-V provides Honda with a smaller crossover. Among enthusiasts, the Civic Type R, Integra Type R, and NSX are particularly important to Honda's performance reputation.

Honda Motorsports

Motorsport is fundamental to Honda's identity, with major programs in Formula 1, MotoGP, IndyCar, Super GT, Super Formula, motocross, and endurance racing. Honda first entered F1 in 1964 and has had several major eras, including its highly successful partnership with McLaren in the late 1980s and early 1990s and its championship-winning partnership with Red Bull, culminating in Max Verstappen's 2021 F1 drivers' championship. In 2026, Honda supplies Aston Martin under the new F1 regulations. In MotoGP, Honda has won numerous premier-class rider and manufacturer championships, while in IndyCar it has won multiple championships and the Indianapolis 500; these racing programs continue to serve as an important testing ground for Honda's engine, hybrid, electrical, and chassis technologies.
