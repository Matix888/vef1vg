MAZDA

History of Mazda

Mazda was founded in Hiroshima, Japan, in 1920, originally as a cork-producing company called Toyo Cork Kogyo. It began producing vehicles with the three-wheeled Mazda-Go in 1931 and eventually became an automobile manufacturer. Mazda became particularly famous for adopting and developing the Wankel rotary engine, which powered iconic cars such as the Cosmo Sport, RX-7, and RX-8. The company also developed a strong reputation for lightweight sports cars, culminating in the launch of the MX-5 Miata in 1989, which became one of the world's best-selling two-seat sports cars.

Current State of Mazda

Today, Mazda is a relatively small global automaker compared with Toyota, Honda, or Volkswagen, but it maintains a distinct identity centered on driving enjoyment, design, and efficient engineering. Its lineup includes gasoline and hybrid-powered vehicles, with major models such as the Mazda3, CX-30, CX-5, CX-50, CX-60, CX-70, CX-80, and CX-90 depending on the market. Mazda has also moved toward electrification while continuing to develop efficient internal-combustion engines, including its Skyactiv technology. Its relationship with Toyota has also become increasingly important, including cooperation on technology and manufacturing.

Most Popular Models

Mazda's most important models today are primarily its SUVs and crossovers, particularly the CX-5, which has become the company's best-selling vehicle globally, along with the CX-30 and CX-50. The Mazda3 remains an important compact car, while the MX-5 Miata is Mazda's most internationally recognizable sports car. The MX-5 is particularly significant because it follows Mazda's philosophy of keeping a sports car simple, lightweight, affordable, and focused on handling rather than maximum horsepower. Historically, the RX-7 and RX-8 were also major enthusiast models because of their distinctive rotary engines.
