SUBARU

History of Subaru

Subaru's history began in 1953 when Fuji Heavy Industries established the Subaru automobile division, with the company tracing its engineering roots to the aircraft industry. Subaru became famous for its boxer engines and symmetrical all-wheel-drive systems, which eventually became central to its identity. The company expanded internationally with models such as the Legacy, Impreza, and Forester, while its partnership with Prodrive and participation in the World Rally Championship turned the Impreza WRX STI into one of the most recognizable Japanese performance cars of the 1990s and 2000s.

Current State of Subaru

Today, Subaru remains a relatively small Japanese automaker but has a particularly strong following in markets such as North America. Its modern identity is built around all-wheel drive, practicality, safety, and outdoor-oriented vehicles, with major models including the Forester, Outback, Crosstrek, Impreza, and WRX. Subaru has also expanded into electrification through models such as the Solterra, developed with Toyota, while continuing to use boxer engines and all-wheel drive across much of its lineup. Toyota is also Subaru's largest strategic partner, owning a significant stake in the company and collaborating on vehicles and technology.

Most Popular Models

The Forester, Outback, and Crosstrek are among Subaru's most important models, combining SUV-like practicality with standard or widely available all-wheel drive. The Forester is particularly associated with Subaru's practical, outdoors-oriented image, while the Outback helped establish the concept of a raised, rugged wagon. The Crosstrek provides a smaller alternative. For enthusiasts, the WRX is Subaru's most important performance model, evolving from the Impreza WRX and retaining a turbocharged boxer engine and all-wheel drive. The BRZ, developed jointly with Toyota, is another notable performance car, using a lightweight rear-wheel-drive layout rather than Subaru's traditional AWD formula.

Subaru Motorsports

Subaru's motorsport reputation was built primarily through rallying, especially the World Rally Championship. The Subaru World Rally Team and its blue-and-gold Impreza WRX STI became icons during the 1990s, with Colin McRae winning the 1995 drivers' championship and Subaru winning the manufacturers' championship three consecutive times from 1995–1997. The Impreza's combination of turbocharged boxer power, sophisticated all-wheel drive, and rally-focused chassis development made it one of the defining performance cars of its era. Subaru has since reduced its factory involvement in top-level international rallying, but the WRX STI legacy remains a major part of the company's performance identity.
