MITSUBISHI

History of Mitsibishi

Mitsubishi Motors began its automotive history with the Mitsubishi Model A in 1917, Japan's first mass-produced passenger car, although Mitsubishi Motors as a separate company was established much later. Over the decades, Mitsubishi became known for practical cars and especially four-wheel-drive technology, eventually producing famous models such as the Galant, Pajero, Lancer, and Lancer Evolution. Motorsport became central to its engineering identity, with the company using rallying and the Dakar Rally to develop its four-wheel-drive, suspension, and durability technologies.

Current State of Mitsubishi

Today, Mitsubishi Motors is a smaller Japanese automaker and is part of the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance. Its modern lineup is concentrated heavily on SUVs, crossovers, and electrified vehicles, with models such as the Outlander, Outlander PHEV, Eclipse Cross, ASX, and Triton/L200 depending on the market. Mitsubishi has particularly emphasized plug-in-hybrid technology and four-wheel-drive systems, while continuing to transition toward broader electrification; the company currently targets a 50% electrified-vehicle sales ratio by fiscal 2030 and 100% by fiscal 2035.

Most Popular Models

The Outlander is one of Mitsubishi's most important modern vehicles, particularly because of its plug-in-hybrid version, while the Triton/L200 pickup is an important model in many international markets. Historically, however, Mitsubishi's most recognizable vehicles are the Pajero/Montero, Lancer, and Lancer Evolution. The Pajero became famous for its durability and off-road capability, while the Lancer Evolution became one of the defining Japanese performance cars of the 1990s and 2000s, combining a turbocharged 2.0-liter engine with sophisticated four-wheel drive and technology developed through rallying.

Mitsubishi Motorsports

Mitsubishi has one of Japan's strongest rallying histories. The Lancer Evolution entered the WRC in the early 1990s and went on to win four consecutive drivers' championships beginning in 1996, helping develop Mitsubishi's sophisticated four-wheel-drive technology. The Pajero/Montero was even more dominant in the Dakar Rally: Mitsubishi won the event repeatedly during the 1990s and 2000s, including seven consecutive victories from 2001–2007 and 12 overall victories during its factory Dakar program. Mitsubishi ended its factory Dakar participation after the 2009 event, but its rally heritage remains one of the defining parts of the brand.
